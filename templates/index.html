Cheztrap is a Chrome extension that tells you whether a website or email is safe.

The internet is a huge part of our lives. You shouldn’t have to browse it on edge.

Cheztrap checks sites and emails, warning you about fake sites, impersonation, and phishing attempts before you enter personal information or click anything. No more stopping to wonder, “Is this safe?”

Most security tools just tell you whether a site/email is “safe” or “unsafe.” Cheztrap tells you the "why."

Along with its verdict, Cheztrap gives you a quick profile of each site: what the company does, who founded it, where it’s based, how much funding it has raised, and how much traffic it gets. That context helps you judge a site for yourself. A brand-new site with very little traffic, for example, deserves a closer look.

Cheztrap also uses existing databases and AI to analyze sites and emails as you open them, so you get answers in seconds. And because it explains what it finds, you gradually learn to spot red flags on your own.

Cheztrap checks each site against multiple trusted threat databases and looks for common scam signals, like how new the domain is, how much traffic it gets, and whether it matches known phishing patterns. AI then pulls the results together into a clear verdict and explains what it found, so you know not just whether a site is risky, but why.

Cheztrap is built to collect as little data as possible. To check a site, it sends the site’s address to threat-intelligence services and an AI model to get a verdict and explanation. For emails, it analyzes only the content needed to run the check. Cheztrap is a play on “cheese trap” and “mousetrap,” and scams often work like bait.

The idea came from my early interest in online scams. I was fascinated by how Remote Access Trojans (RATs) work, which are malicious files disguised as harmless ones. Many Minecraft players lost their accounts to them, which got me interested in online security.

Cheztrap focuses mainly on phishing, but the name stuck.
